# Live Minutes features

The full feature list for the hosted edition at https://minutes.kevinle.tech. The project overview is in the [README](../README.md).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Web app** | Sign up as a Student, Faculty, Staff, or IT account with any email, then confirm a student or work email at your college to join or request an organization. Districts, colleges, and organizations are official: new ones appear only after college IT, district IT, or the platform owner approves them, and look-alike names are flagged. Invites, activity log, and a customizable theme. |
| **Sign-in security** | Optional two-step sign-in with any authenticator app, with ten single-use recovery codes. It is also asked after school sign-in, and a password reset never skips it. My account, Security lists every signed-in device with a sign-out button, and password and two-step changes send a security email. Session cookies are HttpOnly and never stored where page scripts can read them, and sign-in, password reset, and code entry are all rate limited. |
| **Recording notice and policies** | Creating a meeting, scheduling a series, or uploading a recording needs a box confirming that everyone is or was told the meeting is being captured; the confirmation is kept in the audit log. The Terms cover recording consent, a copyright takedown process with a repeat infringer rule, and the 13 and older rule. The Privacy policy says there is no session replay, nothing is sold or shared, Global Privacy Control is honored, and fonts and scripts come only from Live Minutes plus Cloudflare's bot check. |
| **Public homepage** | `/` is always the homepage (`web/src/pages/Home.tsx`), signed in or not: what Live Minutes does, who it is for, how Zoom import works, other features, AI choices, and privacy, with sample-meeting screenshots and links to sign in or create an account (`/login?mode=signup`). Signed-in visitors see Open dashboard instead. The signed-in app starts at `/dashboard` (the Meetings list), which is where sign-in, the sidebar, the logo, the desktop app, and the installed web app open. Sign-in itself lives at `/login`. The site also has home screen icons and a web app manifest, and its link previews show in Live Minutes blue. |
| **Search engines and link previews** | The server fills each public page's head (`server/app/seo.py`): title, description, canonical link, Open Graph and Twitter tags with a preview image from a sample meeting, and SoftwareApplication structured data on the home page. Public pages are the homepage, Privacy, Terms, Accessibility, Support, Zoom help, Downloads, and any organization's public archive. Every other page, including `/dashboard`, is the signed-in app and is sent with `noindex` (header and meta tag) and left out of the sitemap. It is not blocked in robots.txt, so search engines can still read the `noindex`. `/robots.txt` and `/sitemap.xml` (which lists public archives) come from the server. An address that is not a page of the site or the app answers 404 with `noindex` and shows a Page not found card, and `/.well-known/security.txt` gives the security contact. |
| **Personal and School sign-up** | Sign-up asks whether Live Minutes is for Personal use or School, and to confirm the person is 13 or older along with the Terms and Privacy policy (accepting a new version of the terms asks again). Personal accounts, with any email, get their own private workspace as soon as they confirm their email, with full access and their own Zoom connection. School accounts pick Student, Faculty, Staff, or IT and join or request an organization at their college. Personal workspaces belong to no college or district, never appear in the school directory, and keep up to 2 GB of recordings each (a platform setting). They get their own dashboard: no Votes, Funding, Members, or Officers, no member invites (the workspace stays private to its owner), and the owner can delete it. Examples, sample meetings (team check-ins, project planning, study groups, book clubs), the default template, and the AI's instructions are written for personal meetings instead of student government. The platform owner can turn open personal sign-up off and still invite people to personal workspaces. The first choice is free, but switching later to Student needs a confirmed student email, and to Faculty, Staff, or IT a confirmed work email, from a college on Live Minutes (switching to Personal needs none). |
| **Reference transcripts** | Add up to three transcripts of the same meeting from other note-taking apps (Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, tl;dv, Read.ai, Teams, Meet) as .txt, .srt, or .vtt files or pasted text. The AI still drafts from the main transcript and uses these only to check names, numbers, motions, and votes, marking disagreements with [verify]. They are deleted with the meeting, follow transcript retention rules, and are included in data exports. |
| **Shared accounts** | When several people speak from one Zoom account, such as a club's shared account or a room, list it under Settings, General, with the people who use it. The AI works out who is speaking from context, such as "This is Kevin" or "Go ahead, Kevin", and marks anything it cannot tell with [verify]. |
| **Roles** | Platform owner (everything, including billing), district IT (one district), college IT (one college), and inside each organization Full access (advisors and presidents), Runs meetings (secretaries), Member, and Read only. Only college and district IT create or delete organizations unless they allow faculty and staff to create them or positions with the Delete permission to delete them; everyone else requests. IT roles need a confirmed staff email and are only granted downward. See [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md#roles). |
| **Officers** | Each organization has positions (Advisor, President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, Officer, or its own) with a rank and a set of permissions. Terms can be permanent or end on a date, and access ends on its own when a term does. By default the President assigns and removes officers, the Vice President only assigns, and only advisors and IT can change what each position can do. Advisors can review minutes before they are approved. |
| **College directory** | Requesting a new college suggests official names and their email domains from the public university-domains-list (US entries bundled in `server/app/data`, MIT licensed), and the approval screen shows whether the requester's confirmed email matches that college's known domains. |
| **Dashboards** | Platform dashboard for users, organizations, IT roles, requests, colleges, billing, email, security settings, and activity. IT console for district and college IT, limited to their own area. |
| **Meetings** | Start a meeting now or schedule it, with a Zoom link and room. Meetings can repeat weekly, every few weeks, or monthly (such as the first Monday or the last Thursday) and keep their local time across daylight saving changes. A month calendar, "Add to calendar" files, and a private calendar link for Google Calendar, Outlook, or Apple Calendar. Captions from the desktop app start a scheduled meeting when it begins. Scheduling warns when a meeting or a repeating series lands on a federal holiday or the day after Thanksgiving, and This week lists holidays coming up. Meetings that were scheduled but never started move into the minutes list so they can be cleaned up, and Full access members can select meetings there and delete them (nothing under a legal hold can be deleted). |
| **Templates** | Upload your own Word template, a list of topics, or an example of finished minutes for the AI to copy the style of. Twelve built-in styles (formal student government, modern team meeting, simple club, committee working session, board or senate under Robert's Rules, special meeting, one-page brief, executive board, finance committee, event planning, project status update, and one-on-one check-in) open in a designer with a live page preview. Every word can be changed, including the date line, the first and last agenda items, and each section heading; topics can be reordered; the title, subtitle, date, and headings can be left, centered, or right aligned; and the font, accent color, sizes, heading style (underlined, color band, tinted, plain, or small caps), and agenda numbering (1., I., A., a)) are adjustable. A template can also have a classic, color banner, or masthead title; a meeting details table that the Word export fills with the date, start and end times, and location; numbered sections; colored agenda numbers; signature lines for approval; and a page footer with page numbers. The designer opens as a page editor: click any text on the page to type over it, drag topics to reorder them, remove or add sections, add a note under the title or a closing note, and change the font, color, sizes, alignment, and styles from a toolbar with undo and redo, while the parts the AI fills stay locked. A form view is still there. Download it as Word and edit it again later. |
| **Sample meetings** | Secretaries can make 1 to 10 realistic practice meetings at a time from the bottom of the Meetings page (`/dashboard`), short (about 15 minutes), standard (about 45), long (about 90), a mix, or a chosen length up to two hours. Every sample has its own topic and title and no two share a transcript. Each is different: regular, special, budget, workshop, and appointment meetings built on the organization's own template, with made-up members, funding requests, motions that pass, fail, or get tabled, roll-call votes, late arrivals, public comment, off-topic chatter, audio problems, chat, and misheard names. One is upcoming, one is in session, and the rest are finished, some with a draft already written. They are marked Sample and removed in one step. Personal workspaces get personal samples instead: team check-ins, project planning, volunteer, study, neighborhood, and book club meetings with updates, decisions, and action items, about 15 or 25 minutes long. |
| **Draft history** | Every AI draft, save, AI app save, and restore is kept as a version. The History tab lists them like commits, with added and removed counts, shows a line and word level comparison against the version before or the current draft, and lets a secretary restore any version without losing the current one. |
| **Records** | Each meeting keeps an official list of motions with who moved, who seconded, the result, and each person's vote, started from the motions found in the transcript. Search every meeting's minutes, motions, and transcript with links straight to the line, or ask a question and get a short answer that cites its sources. Voting records per person and funding requests tracked from submission to payment and linked to the approving motion, all exportable as CSV. |
| **Sharing** | A plain-language summary of each meeting for students and the public, and translations of the minutes and summary into Spanish, Vietnamese, Chinese, Korean, Tagalog, and more, each with its own Word file. Quick translation runs LibreTranslate on the server for an instant, private first pass; the AI translation is the careful one. Every Word file gets a title and language, and an accessibility check covers headings, image descriptions, table headers, spacing, text size, color contrast, and link text. |
| **Notifications** | An inbox with an unread count for draft minutes that are ready (or failed), minutes waiting for review, review results, a reminder the day before each scheduled meeting with its Zoom link, officer terms starting or ending, and a security notice (also emailed) whenever a new AI app connects to the account. A This week page summarizes each organization's last and next seven days. Each kind can also come by email; the weekly summary email is off unless a person turns it on. |
| **District setup** | District IT can turn on school sign-in by entering their Microsoft Entra tenant IDs or Google Workspace domains; people from those directories get an account, a confirmed school email, and a student or staff type the first time they sign in. District and college IT keep a shared template library that organizations copy from, and owners can invite up to 200 people at once from a pasted list or CSV. |
| **Records management** | District IT sets retention rules (remove never-approved meetings, transcripts of approved minutes, or old approved minutes after a set time) and places legal holds on the district, a college, or an organization; nothing under a hold can be deleted by anyone or by retention. A full export builds one ZIP of every organization's members, officer terms, meetings, minutes, votes, transcripts, translations, funding, templates, and activity, without AI keys or tokens. |
| **Reports and paperwork** | Monthly usage for each district, college, and the whole platform: meetings, minutes approved, days to approval, active organizations and people, AI calls and cost, translations, and an adjustable time-saved estimate, with CSV download. HECVAT Lite answers, an accessibility conformance report, a FERPA data protection agreement template, and a subprocessor list ship with the server for district review. |
| **Backups** | District and college IT connect their own S3-compatible storage (Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2, Backblaze B2, Wasabi, DigitalOcean, Google Cloud, Linode, or a self-hosted MinIO the server owner allows) or Azure Blob Storage, choose which records go there (minutes, transcripts, translations, members, funding, templates, activity), and schedule daily or weekly copies. The server itself makes a nightly database and file backup. |
| **Policies** | Privacy policy, Terms of use, and an accessibility statement, linked from every page and accepted at sign-up. |
| **Recordings** | Upload a meeting after the fact (recording, Zoom transcript, chat), or paste a Zoom share link to copy everything from a connected Zoom account. Recordings upload in pieces so large files work, and play in the browser beside a transcript that follows along and jumps to any line, with downloads of the recording, transcript, captions, and subtitles. A Recordings page lists every recording with a download, and secretaries can download them all as one ZIP; district and college IT can download every recording in their area from the IT console. Recordings are not part of automatic backups. Settings, Zoom shows a checklist that reads the connected Zoom account and flags cloud recording, audio transcripts, saved chat, automatic cloud recording, and automated captions that are off, with links to the Zoom settings to fix them. |
| **Privacy** | Every meeting, recording, transcript, and set of minutes is private to the organization's members and the college and district staff who manage it. An organization's owner can turn on a public archive (/archive/<org id>) that shows only approved minutes, their plain-language summaries, and a Word file of each; drafts, recordings, transcripts, votes, and members are never public. |
| **Unfinished business** | New meetings list the organization's tabled motions and motions with no recorded result from the last six months; the ones ticked go into the meeting's notes so the draft carries them forward. |
| **Zoom auto-import** | Optional per organization: when the connected Zoom account finishes a cloud recording (`recording.completed` or `recording.transcript_completed`), Live Minutes makes the meeting, imports captions or transcript and chat once, starts the draft if an AI is set up, and notifies the people who run meetings. It fetches the recording through its own OAuth connection, never from the webhook's links. |
| **Billing** | Plans and subscriptions per district, college, or organization, with recurring revenue, renewals, and seat use. Records only; no payment processor is connected yet. |
| **Desktop app** | Windows installer, downloadable from the site's Downloads page (/download) with its SHA-256. Connects to minutes.kevinle.tech by default (File, Server settings for a district's own copy) and opens with a three-step welcome tour the first time, which the sign-in page offers again inside the app (Take the welcome tour). The main window has a dark title bar whose File, View, and Help buttons open the app menus. Live capture (Ctrl+Shift+C) walks through connecting with a capture token, choosing a meeting, and joining Zoom inside the app, then shows a live timer, lines sent, the latest caption, and the draft status. A floating bar stays on top of Zoom while capturing. Updates itself (electron-updater, generic feed at `/api/downloads/latest.yml`): it checks 15 seconds after starting and every 6 hours, downloads in the background, checks the SHA-512 in the feed, and asks to restart, never during a live capture. Help, Check for updates, or the button in Live capture, checks on demand. Not code-signed yet. |
| **Browser extension** | The same caption reader for people who join Zoom in Chrome or Edge. Offered as a zip on the Downloads page and headed to Microsoft Edge Add-ons (listing text in [docs/EDGE-LISTING.md](EDGE-LISTING.md)). It only reaches minutes.kevinle.tech or the desktop app on the same computer. |
| **Free AI** | Every account can use AI with no key: open models run on the Live Minutes server through Ollama (Google's Gemma 4 E4B, recommended, and Qwen 3.5 4B), so meeting text never goes to an AI company. They handle minutes, summaries, translations, record questions, and the assistant. Requests wait in one shared line on the server, one at a time: questions, summaries, translations, and the assistant go ahead of minutes drafts, but a draft that has started finishes first. Minutes are drafted after the meeting ends in their own worker thread, so free drafts never hold up anyone's own AI. Other requests come back as `202 {pending}` (Cloudflare closes requests after about 100 seconds); the page polls `/api/free-ai/runs/<id>` every 10 seconds and repeats the request with `X-Free-AI-Run` once it is done. Each request shows a progress bar: the worker streams the model's output and saves a real checkpoint every 15 seconds and when the model finishes reading and starts writing, the estimate uses reading and writing speeds learned from earlier runs, and the bar moves smoothly between checkpoints without passing the next one. Each person can have two free requests waiting at once. Setup is in [docs/DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md#free-built-in-ai). |
| **AI** | Claude, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Groq, Grok, Hugging Face (open models through its Inference Providers), LM Studio, Ollama, or any OpenAI-compatible server. A district can share its AIs with colleges, a college with organizations or people, and anyone can add their own key, with a button to each provider's key page; any AI can be tested, edited (name, model, key, or server), or removed later. Each task (minutes, questions, translation, summaries) can use a different AI with its own editable prompt. Usage and estimated cost are tracked, with monthly limits per organization. No key needed: connect an OpenRouter account with one click, or connect Claude (website, desktop, phone, or Claude Code), ChatGPT, Gemini (Spark custom apps on Google AI Pro or Ultra), Grok, Le Chat (free plan included), or Perplexity to Live Minutes through its MCP server by signing in, and draft with your own plan (those apps' sites are on the MCP origin allowlist and named on the sign-in page); each meeting has Claude, ChatGPT, and Grok links that open your app with the drafting request already filled in, plus a copy button for apps without prefill links. A chat assistant on every page answers questions and suggests changes (assign an officer for six months, schedule a meeting, invite someone), and nothing happens until you press Approve. It has only your permissions, never deletes anything, and shows this month's AI use against the limit. Every AI feature is hardened against prompt injection and runaway use (see [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md#ai-safety-owasp-top-10-for-llm-applications-2025)), and [docs/AI-REFERENCE.md](AI-REFERENCE.md) explains the website to AIs. See [the AI guide](AI-GUIDE.md). |
| **Hosting** | One Docker image. Districts can self-host with Docker Compose (optionally behind a Cloudflare Tunnel), or run one shared server on any container platform. Azure is the planned district home. |
| **Security** | Confirmed emails, strict passwords checked against known breaches, progressive sign-in lockout, optional Cloudflare Turnstile, strict CSP and HSTS, hash-pinned dependencies, and security scans on every push. See [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md). |
